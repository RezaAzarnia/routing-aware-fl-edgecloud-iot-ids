\section{Related work and research gap}
\label{sec:related}
\emph{Scope note.} Of the 28 application-level sources cited below (foundational references are listed separately), we read the full text (partly, in some cases a preprint or an accepted manuscript) of 11 and only the abstract or an abstract fragment of 17; publisher access restrictions prevented a complete full-text review. Statements about sources read at abstract level are marked ``(abstract level)'' and are limited to what the abstract says. The gap statement below is limited to the sources examined.

\textbf{Federated intrusion detection for IoT.} A survey \cite{agrawal2022flids} and several studies apply FL to IoT intrusion detection \cite{albanbay2025ids,alghadi2025dos,peng2025fdids,rahmati2025cybersecurity,albogami2025deepfl}; recent proposals add hierarchical multi-model aggregation \cite{latif2026multimodel}, temporal gradient auditing with secure aggregation against poisoning \cite{soomro2026securedyn}, or knowledge distillation for non-IID data \cite{peng2025fdids}. In the sources whose full text we could read, these works focus on the detector, the poisoning defence or the privacy layer, and none models how the delivery path or a client-selection policy shapes which updates reach the aggregator. For \cite{albanbay2025ids,alghadi2025dos} we read only the abstracts (abstract level), which describe a model and data-scale study and a feature-selection and model comparison, respectively, and mention neither a delivery model nor a selection policy.

\textbf{Robust aggregation.} Median-based and distance-based rules, and attacks against them, are well studied \cite{blanchard2017krum,yin2018byzantine,fang2020local}. Recent Byzantine-robust schemes address heterogeneous data \cite{zhang2026malicious,zhu2026bpflh} (the first at abstract level), and others combine robustness with secure aggregation or secure two-party computation \cite{liu2026anonymous,shu2026trustworthy} (abstract level); joint robustness and privacy in IoT edge settings has been pursued by pairing local-DP noise with statistical filtering and client reputation \cite{s2dfl2026} (abstract level). The median/MAD filter used here is a classical rule built on a robust scale estimate \cite{rousseeuw1993mad}; we do not claim a new estimator. Server momentum follows FedAvgM \cite{hsu2019measuring}; adaptive server optimisers more generally are studied in \cite{reddi2021adaptive}.

\textbf{Fault tolerance and reliability.} Fault-tolerant work in edge FL includes hierarchical multicenter FL with fault-tolerance mechanisms \cite{chen2025hierarchical} and FL with edge servers and a pre-trained model for fault tolerance and latency \cite{haripriya2025fault} (both abstract level), and a modular architecture for dynamic edge environments with benign worker failures \cite{almeida2025modular}. FL-trained regression models have also been used for fault-aware scheduling of IoT requests onto virtual machines in cloud data centres; the fault tolerance there concerns the scheduler, not FL training \cite{aburukba2025scheduling} (abstract level). ``Reliability'' is used in other senses by multi-server secure FL with configurable edge-node reliability against collusion and poisoning \cite{liu2026reliability} and by FL under clustered data in a three-tier IIoT architecture \cite{tang2026fedcst} (abstract fragments only). Our median-based surrogate imputation of missing updates is evaluated here only as a component and is not compared with these mechanisms.

\textbf{Network-aware, edge and resource-aware FL.} FL has been used to \emph{learn} routing policies \cite{abdallah2026routing,zhang2026adaptive,bhardwaj2024routing} (the first and third at abstract level), and to schedule tasks onto edge nodes \cite{alanazi2026edgeguard}; edge--cloud and resource-aware FL architectures target quality of service and resource constraints \cite{zhou2024qos,liu2024iiot,mughal2024adaptive} (the first at abstract level). In the abstracts, and in the full text of \cite{zhang2026adaptive}, FL is used to produce routing decisions; whether and how these studies model the delivery of the FL updates themselves is not stated in the parts we read. We study how a routing and delivery model shapes \emph{which client updates reach the aggregator}. The effect of the selection policy on bias and participation is studied in FL client-selection work \cite{cho2022biased}; we measure participation jointly with delivery metrics in one simulator.

\textbf{Privacy.} Central Gaussian perturbation with an RDP accountant \cite{abadi2016dpsgd,mcmahan2018dprnn,mironov2017renyi,zhu2019poisson} gives a formal guarantee only under its assumptions (Poisson subsampling, data-independent clipping and weights). Other privacy layers use secure aggregation, secure multi-party computation (with a blockchain in \cite{geng2024smpc}) or homomorphic encryption \cite{bonawitz2017secagg,geng2024smpc,lan2025ckks} (the last two at abstract level); Shah et al.\ report Byzantine attacks on IDS data together with DP and secure aggregation \cite{shah2026trisafe} (abstract level) and therefore overlap with our robustness and privacy elements in kind; whether their mechanisms differ from ours cannot be judged from the abstract. Our Gaussian arms violate the accountant's assumptions and are reported as an empirical noise-injection study only.

\textbf{Closest studies we could read in full.} Almeida et al.\ \cite{almeida2025modular} use UNSW-NB15 and benign worker failures in a modular FL architecture, but have no packet-level delivery model, route-scored selection, imputation of missing updates or participation-fairness measure. Mughal et al.\ \cite{mughal2024adaptive} select nodes by a static network-quality score without a delivery model or fairness measurement. Latif and Djenouri \cite{latif2026multimodel} use 50 clients with statistical filtering of poisoned updates and latency reporting, but a static client-to-edge assignment. Zhang et al.\ \cite{zhang2026adaptive} train a routing predictor with FL (all nodes participate), and Alanazi and Alanazi \cite{alanazi2026edgeguard} schedule tasks onto nodes with random client sampling. These are the nearest neighbours in our reading; none jointly measures the elements of our gap statement below.

\subsection*{Research gap and what this paper does (and does not) contribute}
Within the sources examined (11 read in full or in part and 17 at abstract level among the application-level studies), we did not find a controlled study that measures, under one simulator and one locked protocol, (a) the trade-off between delivery reliability and participation fairness of route-scored client selection, (b) the single-factor effect of the delivery model, surrogate imputation, weighting and momentum on a median/MAD-filtered FL-IDS pipeline, and (c) the behaviour of the filter as a function of the number of genuine updates, \emph{including negative results}. Absence from an abstract is not proof of absence from the paper, and no systematic literature search was performed; this is a statement about the sources we checked, not a claim of priority. We separate three kinds of contribution:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Implementation/engineering:} a deterministic, resumable, hash-locked simulator and an audit trail (original versus audited metrics).
\item \textbf{Systems integration:} the combination of route-scored selection, packet-level delivery, filtering, imputation, momentum and optional noise; combination alone is not claimed as novel.
\item \textbf{Experimentally established (exploratory, $n=5$):} the findings in Sec.~\ref{sec:results} -- no new estimator, no privacy guarantee, no general Byzantine robustness.
\end{itemize}
