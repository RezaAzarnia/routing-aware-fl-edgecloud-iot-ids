## Non-oracle baseline `history_ucb` (`src/history_selector.py`)
* **Inputs** only: per client, number of selections, number of updates actually *received* by the server, EWMA (α=0.3) of the delay of *received* updates; round number; seed. No access to the network object (AST-tested), true stability/latency/security, attacker identity, labels or future outcomes (`observe` accepts only current-round selected clients; decision at round t is a function of history through t−1, tested).
* **Cold start / unknown clients:** never-selected → index +∞ (optimistic initialisation): all 50 clients are tried in the first 5 rounds (10 per round), ties broken by a seeded permutation. **Exploration vs exploitation:** UCB1 bonus c·√(ln t / n_i), c=√2 (canonical, not tuned). **Success estimate:** received/selected. **Latency:** min–max of the delay EWMA among clients with an observation; neutral 0.5 otherwise. **Security:** unobservable, weight dropped; score = (0.4·latency + 0.3·UCB-success)/0.7. **Determinism:** seeded; identical history ⇒ identical selection.
* Historical policies (`top_k`, `coverage_aware`, `uniform_random`) are unchanged and bit-identical (tests vs frozen tree).

## Correlated scenario (`routing.network_data_coupling`, default 0)
Driver = each client's **minority-class share** (computed by the simulator from the partition, never given to a policy). The initially generated link sets are **permuted** across clients so that clients with higher driver get worse composite link quality (stability − latency term): Pearson-controlled mix `ρ·z(driver)+√(1−ρ²)·noise`, ρ=0.8 (Spearman −0.8 to −0.9 with the composite index; −0.6 with stability alone — measured). Matched control: ρ=0 is bit-identical to the historical network; the coupled network is the **same** generated network relabeled (same marginals, same later RNG use). Attackers: a fixed client set drawn from a seed-only stream independent of network and policy, so no policy can see or correlate with it; exposure then depends only on who the policy selects.

## Matched design (40 runs)
4 policies {oracle_topk, oracle_coverage, uniform, history_ucb} × 2 scenarios {independent, coupled} × seeds 22–26 (fresh), UNSW-NB15, train/validation data only, CPU. Verified by test: the 8 configs differ **only** in selection policy, coupling strength and labels; they differ from the locked Phase 6C `ctl_delivery_coupled` config only by the new flags (holdout 0.2, policy/coupling) and natural attacker assignment. Same partitions (seed), initialization, attack, training, evaluation partitions, matched traces. Residual confound (declared): holdout removes 20 % of each client's training data in *all* arms.

## Outcome metrics and validity
Measured: PDR (first transmission), **actual update-delivery ratio** (received/selected), delay (received updates), selection Gini, never-selected, longest selection gap, malicious share of selected, best/last AUC and F1. **Client recall:** valid **only** with the per-client holdout (never used for training, selection or thresholds): worst, p10, mean over clients with ≥10 attack holdout samples (others excluded and counted). **Not valid** and not used: the Phase 6C `client_level_recall.csv` (scores each client's own *training* data, optimistic for selected clients; this also qualifies the earlier "0.975 vs 0.976" statement, which should be read as unproven). Holdout samples share their client's distribution (not independent devices).

## Pre-registered hypotheses (directional expectations, not success criteria)
H1 history_ucb − oracle_topk, independent, update-delivery ratio: negative. H2 same, Gini: negative. H3 oracle_topk − uniform, coupled, p10 client recall: negative. H4 history_ucb − oracle_topk, coupled, p10 recall: positive. Secondary: all pairwise policy contrasts per scenario for every metric, and policy×scenario interactions. **Statistics:** paired by seed, mean, SD, d_z, t-CI (df 4), seeds favouring each arm, exact sign-flip p (min 0.0625); no correction; exploratory only; effect size and 5/5 consistency are the evidence. Limits: one network family, one coupling mechanism/strength, UNSW only, simulation; null or opposite results are reported as such.
